M8.5 — MODERN-LAYOUT RESILIENCE

Not perfect, but navigable

A small slice added after pointing the engine at a modern flex/grid portal. The goal is not a pixel-perfect render — it is to stop the two most violent failures so a modern page degrades gracefully instead of detonating: images that balloon to their intrinsic size, and content that bleeds across the page.

Percentage-sized images

Each icon below is the same 256×256 image, sized with width: 100% of a fixed 44px cell; the height follows the image’s intrinsic aspect ratio. A replaced element now resolves a percentage against its containing block instead of falling back to its intrinsic pixel size.

Five 44px cells, not five 256px images shoving the row off-screen.

Before this change, a %/rem width on an <img> or SVG resolved to its bare magnitude and the element fell back to the media’s natural size — exactly why a portal’s icon menu rendered as a wall of giant circles.

object-fit

The same square image placed in a wide 160×90 frame under each object-fit value. A replaced element now scales its content to fit the box instead of always stretching it.

fill (stretch)
contain
cover (crops)
none

fill distorts the circle to the frame, contain letterboxes it whole, cover fills the frame and clips the overflow, and none paints it at intrinsic size (also clipped to the frame). Positioning is centered; object-position is a small follow-up.

overflow: hidden clipping

The solid card is overflow: hidden and shorter than its content, so the content is clipped to the card instead of spilling across the page.

This paragraph is taller than the 90 pixel card that contains it. Because the card is overflow: hidden, everything past its bottom edge is clipped away rather than painting over the text and boxes that follow it on the page.
The dashed box has default overflow: visible, so the same tall content simply makes it grow — nothing is clipped here, for contrast.

Clipping applies only to the overflow: hidden shorthand (both axes). Scroll containers, scrollbars, and overflow: auto/scroll offsets remain M10 work — this slice only stops the bleed.
